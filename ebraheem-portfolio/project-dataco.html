REC-001 — Best Graduation Project, NTI Data Analysis Track

DataCo Smart Supply Chain for Big Data Analysis

A 5-member capstone team was handed a 180,000-row, 53-variable retail supply chain dataset and one question: sales are $36.78M, so why does profit look 50% smaller than it should? I led the sales & profitability analysis workstream.

Role
Lead Sales & Profitability Analyst
Team
5 members
Dataset
180K+ records · 53 variables
Tools
SQL · Power BI · Star Schema
Problem

$36.78M in sales, but the profit line didn't match.

The dataset covered a global retail supply chain, but leadership had no reliable way to see where the 50% profit gap was actually coming from — was it discounting, fraud, or bad market allocation? All three turned out to be true.

Approach

Model it, then interrogate it with SQL.

I engineered a star-schema data model to make 180K+ records and 53 variables queryable at speed, then wrote SQL using window functions, CTEs and multi-table joins to isolate exactly which transactions were destroying margin. From there I ran three separate diagnostics:

  • A discount-impact analysis testing whether bigger discounts actually drove more volume.
  • A payment-method correlation check against high-demand product orders.
  • A geographic price-sensitivity analysis comparing discount-heavy markets against premium ones.
Result

A capped discount policy, a fraud flag, and a market reallocation — worth $2.2M+.

The SQL analysis traced $3.73M in lost revenue directly to inefficient discount policy. Impact analysis found a 10–13% "golden ratio" for discounts — beyond that, a 50% discount produced no extra volume, just lost margin. I proposed a 5% discount cap, which modeled out to $2.2M in net profit savings.

Separately, I found a 100% correlation between high-demand product fraud and "Bank Transfer" payments — a security loophole worth $20K+ in fictitious revenue — and recommended payment-gateway authentication fixes. A geographic pricing review also flagged that marketing spend should shift from high-discount, low-revenue Central Asia toward premium, higher-margin Northern Europe.
